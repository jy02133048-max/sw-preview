The method behind the match

Most shops sell you the stone you already like.
We read your chart first — then argue with you about it.

No stars, no month-by-month forecast. Just the same structural reading our Studio runs on your birth details.

Your birth details can be written as a small table of elements. Some run heavy, some run short. That balance is what we read — and it’s how we decide which stone actually belongs on your wrist.

A method, not a mood

Fixed inputs, fixed rules. The same birth details always produce the same chart — nothing is improvised.

Audited against the classics

The rules trace to classical sources. Where sources disagree, we follow the reading they share — not a hunch.

No fortune-telling

We don’t predict events or promise outcomes. We read which elements are short, and which stone supports them.

Step one

Your birth details become a chart.

Year, month, day, and hour — four parts of one moment. Read together, they lay out as your birth chart: the starting table everything else is read from.

There’s nothing mystical in this first step. A date and a time get converted onto the traditional calendar and split into four columns — the four parts of your chart. It is arithmetic, done the same way every time. Two people born in the same hour get the same chart.

Step two

Your chart carries your elements.

Every part of the chart carries an element. They aren’t random labels — each one feeds the next and keeps another in check.

Wood feeds Fire, Fire makes Earth, Earth bears Metal, Metal holds Water, Water grows Wood. The dashed lines are the counter-balance. A healthy chart keeps these in rough proportion.

Step three

Some elements run heavy. Some run short.

Tally your elements across the chart and you get a balance — rarely even. One or two usually dominate; one or two are thin. The thin ones are the elements a stone can actually support.

“We don’t read the loud elements. We read the quiet one — the one with room for a stone.”

This is the part most shops skip. They sell you the stone you already like, or the one trending this month. We’d rather look at the chart first. If your Fire is thin, a Fire stone has somewhere to go. If your Metal is already heavy, more Metal just adds noise — and we’ll say so.

Step four

Crystals carry elemental character, too.

A stone’s colour, mineral, and origin give it an element of its own. So the right stone isn’t the prettiest one — it’s the one that supports the element your chart is short on.

A worked example · Amethyst

Why amethyst reads as a Fire stone.

Amethyst is purple quartz — silicon dioxide (SiO₂), Mohs 7, the February birthstone. Its deep violet comes from iron and natural irradiation underground, not dye. That warm purple reads as a Fire stone. So if a chart runs short on Fire, amethyst has a real place on the wrist. If a chart already runs hot, we’d point you somewhere cooler instead — that’s the honest version of matching.
